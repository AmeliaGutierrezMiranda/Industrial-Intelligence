# Equipment → Skills → Job Titles Matrix

When I recruit in manufacturing, I don't want to stop at the job title. Titles change from company to company. Equipment, processes and hands-on skills usually tell me much more about what someone actually knows how to do.

This is a working recruiter reference — not an engineering manual. I use it to ask better questions, recognize transferable experience and widen a search without watering down the requirements.

## Machining & CNC

| Equipment / Technology | Skills I Listen For | Common Job Titles | Transferable Environments |
|---|---|---|---|
| CNC mills & lathes | Setup, offsets, tool changes, feeds & speeds, blueprint reading, inspection | CNC Operator, CNC Machinist, Setup Technician, CNC Programmer | Aerospace, automotive, medical devices, metal fabrication, industrial equipment |
| Haas, Mazak, Okuma, DMG Mori and similar CNC platforms | Machine setup, controls, tooling, troubleshooting, production machining | CNC Machinist, CNC Setup Machinist, CNC Programmer | Precision machining, aerospace, defense, automotive, contract manufacturing |
| G-code / M-code | Editing programs, offsets, tool paths, troubleshooting | CNC Programmer, CNC Machinist, Manufacturing Technician | Machine shops, production machining, advanced manufacturing |
| CAM software | Toolpaths, programming, machining strategy | CNC Programmer, Manufacturing Engineer, Applications Engineer | Precision machining, tooling, aerospace, industrial manufacturing |

**Recruiter note:** "CNC experience" is too broad by itself. I want to know whether someone loaded parts, operated the machine, performed setups, edited programs or programmed from scratch. Those are very different levels of experience.

**Recruiter note:** When someone says they "worked with PLCs," I ask what that means. Did they reset faults? Troubleshoot I/O? Read ladder logic? Modify programs? Build controls systems? The phrase alone doesn't tell me enough.

## How I Use This Matrix

I use the matrix in both directions.

**Starting with a job:**

Job title → actual process → equipment → required skill level → adjacent titles → transferable industries → target employers.

**Starting with a candidate:**

Candidate title → equipment actually used → level of responsibility → underlying skills → other environments where those skills make sense.

That second direction matters. Industrial talent is easy to overlook when we search only for exact titles or exact industries.

## What I Don't Assume

Seeing a machine, software package or process on a resume doesn't automatically mean proficiency. I still want to know:

- How often did you use it?
- What did you personally do with it?
- What could you troubleshoot without calling someone else?
- Were you operating, setting up, programming, maintaining or engineering around it?
- What happened when the process went down?

The goal isn't to turn recruiting into engineering. It is to understand enough of the work to stop treating every keyword as equal.
