# The Workforce You Won't Find on LinkedIn

**I recruit nationally, but I build each search locally.**

That means I don't assume the best candidate is sitting on LinkedIn waiting for a recruiter message.

In manufacturing, skilled trades and production, a large part of the workforce has a limited digital footprint. Some candidates are on Indeed. Some are in Facebook job groups. Some are referred by a coworker, relative or friend. Some respond faster to a text or voice message than to a polished recruiting email.

If I want to understand a labor market, I have to understand how people in that market actually look for work.

## The Informal Labor Market

One of the biggest mistakes in national recruiting is treating the online candidate database as if it represents the full workforce.

It doesn't.

In many industrial markets, especially for production, machining, maintenance, fabrication and other hands-on roles, the labor market also lives in:

- Local Facebook job groups
- Hispanic and bilingual employment groups
- Community pages
- Referrals
- Trade schools and technical colleges
- Former coworkers
- Family networks
- Text messages
- Voice notes
- Word of mouth

These channels matter because digital footprint is not the same thing as workforce availability.

A candidate can have ten years of strong manufacturing experience and almost no LinkedIn presence.

## Why I Use Facebook Groups

I join local and Hispanic job-search groups because they give me a different view of the market.

I can see:

- What types of jobs people are actively asking for
- What pay rates get attention
- Which shifts are difficult to fill
- Which companies people recognize
- How far workers are willing to commute
- Whether transportation is becoming a barrier
- Which industries are hiring from the same labor pool
- What questions candidates ask before they are willing to apply

That is useful market intelligence.

It also helps me understand whether the problem is really a candidate shortage or whether the recruiting strategy is simply not reaching the right population.

## Voice Messages Build Trust Faster

When candidates message me through Facebook, I often answer with a voice clip.

It is simple, but it changes the interaction.

Instead of another recruiter sending a scripted message, they hear a real person explain the opportunity, answer the question and communicate naturally.

For candidates who are more comfortable communicating verbally, or in Spanish, that can remove a lot of unnecessary friction.

I am still representing an employer professionally. I am just meeting the candidate where they are.

## Hispanic Workforce Outreach

Hispanic workers are an important part of the manufacturing and production workforce in many U.S. markets, but traditional sourcing channels do not always reflect that workforce accurately.

Recruiting into those communities requires more than translating a job advertisement into Spanish.

It requires listening.

I pay attention to how people describe their work, what they call the machines they operate, how they hear about opportunities, what schedules matter to their families and which employers already have credibility in the community.

Language helps. Cultural familiarity helps. But consistency matters more than either one.

If candidates learn that I answer questions, explain the process and stay accessible, referrals start to happen naturally.

## National Search, Local Build

When I enter a new market, I want to understand more than the number of resumes inside a radius.

I want to know:

**Where does the workforce live?**

**Where do they currently work?**

**What equipment and processes are common in the area?**

**What is a realistic commute?**

**What shifts and wage ranges move people?**

**Which employers compete for the same skills?**

**Where do these workers actually look for jobs?**

**What communities and networks influence referrals?**

The answers are different in Wisconsin, Pennsylvania, California and every other market.

That is why I recruit nationally, but I build each search locally.

## What This Changes for the Client

This approach gives me something more useful to bring back than, "I can't find candidates."

I can explain what the market is telling us.

Maybe the pay is below where experienced machinists are already working.

Maybe the commute radius looks reasonable on a map but crosses a line local workers rarely travel for work.

Maybe the job title is different from what competing employers use.

Maybe qualified workers are present, but they are not on LinkedIn.

Maybe the strongest pool is bilingual production talent that can be trained into machine operation.

Those are recruiting insights that can change the search.

## My Goal

My job is not just to locate resumes.

It is to understand the labor market well enough to know where the workforce is, how to reach it and what the market is telling the employer.

That is the part of recruiting I think of as **Industrial Intelligence**.
