# Recruiter Field Guide

These are the questions I want answered before I assume I understand an industrial role.

## About the Operation

- What does the facility actually make or do?
- What part of the process will this person support?
- What does a normal day look like on the floor?
- Is the environment automated, mechanical, manual or a mix?
- What causes the most downtime or production pressure?

## About the Equipment

- What machines or systems will this person touch?
- Which equipment experience is required versus preferred?
- Are there specific controls, brands or platforms that matter?
- Can someone transfer in from similar equipment?
- What troubleshooting ability is expected on day one?

## About the Skills

- What can the company teach?
- What cannot be taught quickly enough for this role?
- Is direct industry experience truly required?
- What adjacent industries have produced successful hires before?
- What makes someone strong after six months in the job?

## About the Team

- Who does this person report to?
- Who do they work beside every day?
- Is the team experienced enough to train someone?
- Is this an individual contributor, lead or hands-on leadership role?
- Why is the position open?

## About the Schedule

- What shift is it really?
- Is overtime expected, occasional or constant?
- Are weekends part of the normal schedule?
- How stable is the schedule?
- Is there flexibility for the right candidate?

## About Compensation

- What is the realistic hiring range?
- What experience earns the top of the range?
- Is there shift differential, bonus or overtime?
- What benefits materially change the value of the opportunity?
- Is relocation available for harder-to-find talent?

## About the Market

- Which nearby employers have similar equipment or processes?
- Where have previous hires come from?
- How far do employees typically commute?
- Which technical schools or training programs feed the area?
- Is the local workforce active on LinkedIn, or do I need to look elsewhere?

## Candidate Conversations

I also want to hear how candidates describe the work in their own language.

Questions I use include:

- Walk me through the equipment you work on most often.
- When something goes down, what are you personally responsible for troubleshooting?
- What type of production environment are you used to?
- What machines could you walk up to and be comfortable around tomorrow?
- What have you learned on the job that is not obvious from your resume?
- What type of environment do you not want to go back to?

The point is not to turn the recruiter into the technical expert.

The point is to understand enough to recognize the technical expert when you talk to one.
